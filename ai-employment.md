# The Business Case

We have seen one AI executive after another brag that AI will displace enormous numbers of workers.  Why do they say this, given the backlash it produces?

We see that the goal is Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).  Why? Aren't bespoke solutions to specific business and science problems more achievable at much lower cost?

Answer: The only financial justification for the current level of investment and the focus on AGI is mass replacement of workers.  This may not be achieved -- but let's be clear, that's the goal.  AI is not just another app to be "monetized".

# Where AI Is Eliminating or Shrinking Jobs

## The Pattern: Structured, Repetitive, Entry-Level Work

- **Junior software developers** — entry-level postings fell **67%** between 2023–2024; workers aged 22–25 saw employment drop ~20% from its 2022 peak
- **Media & creative production** — job postings at decade lows; graphic artist roles down 33% in 2025 alone; AI writes articles, generates images, manages social media
- **Customer service** — ~80,000 US positions eliminated 2022–2024 as chatbots displace call center agents
- **Financial data processing** — McKinsey estimates 70% of tasks automatable; analyst headcount shrinking at major Wall Street firms
- **Data entry & admin** — average salaries fell from $38K to $33K (2024–2026); 65–80% of roles projected gone by 2028

<div class="callout"><strong>Key finding (Harvard Business School, 2025):</strong> Job postings for structured/repetitive roles fell 13% after ChatGPT's launch; postings for analytical, technical, and creative roles <em>rose 20%</em>.</div>

# Workplace Surveillance: Pressure, Injury & Automation Risk

## AI as a Management Tool — With a Dark Side

- **74%** of US employers now use digital tracking tools (screen monitoring, biometrics, location data)
- **59%** of surveilled employees report stress or anxiety; stress rates run 45% in high-surveillance settings vs. 28% in low-surveillance ones
- **Amazon** is the documented extreme: injury rates significantly above industry average, driven by algorithmic quotas that auto-generate discipline and termination without manager input
  - Nearly **70%** of Amazon warehouse workers report taking unpaid time off due to pain or exhaustion in the past month

---

- **The deeper problem:** surveillance data maps exactly *how* workers do their jobs — creating a training dataset for the robots and AI that will eventually replace them
- Workers are simultaneously being pushed to peak output *and* being digitized for replacement

<div class="callout"><strong>US GAO (2025):</strong> Confirmed that digital surveillance tools <em>"can increase workers' risk of injuries by pushing them to move faster to meet productivity metrics."</em></div>

# Where AI Is Helping: Augmentation, Not Replacement

## The Radiology Case — and the Broader Pattern

**Radiology** has become the textbook example of AI augmentation done right:
- BLS projects **5% job growth** through 2034 — above the national average
- Residency positions hit a **record 1,208** in 2025, up 4% year-over-year; vacancy rates at all-time highs
- Average radiologist income: **$520,000** — 48% higher than in 2015
- Geoffrey Hinton, who famously predicted AI would replace radiologists, walked back the claim in 2025, saying AI will make them "more efficient and accurate"

---

**Other fields seeing genuine augmentation:**

<!-- _class: progress bigtable -->

| Field | AI Role | Human Role |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Discovery, contract review, document analysis | Strategy, advocacy, judgment |
| Mental health | Early-warning triage, pattern monitoring | Therapeutic relationship, care |
| Cybersecurity | Threat detection at scale | Architecture, policy, response |
| Healthcare broadly | Transcription, diagnostics support | Patient care, clinical judgment |

<div class="callout"><strong>The dividing line</strong> is not industry — it's whether a job's core value is <em>information processing</em> (at risk) or <em>judgment, accountability, and human connection</em> (augmentable).</div>

# A Case Study in Disruption: Musicians & AI

## Streaming Royalties Under Pressure

- **~28%** of new Spotify uploads were AI-generated as of 2025 — and rising
- AI tracks topped Spotify's **Viral 50** chart in November 2025 for multiple weeks
- Streaming platforms have a **finite royalty pool** — every AI track that earns plays dilutes what human musicians receive
- CISAC projects a **€4 billion annual loss** to music creators by 2028, with AI representing 60% of music library revenues

**Who is most exposed:**
- Independent artists with no label infrastructure
- Session musicians and sync composers (TV/film/ad music) — exactly the use cases AI handles best
- Emerging artists trying to build audiences through streaming

---

**What remains more resilient:**
- Established artists with loyal audiences and touring revenue
- Live performance — essentially AI-proof
- Human authenticity as a differentiator (an emerging anti-algorithm counterculture is growing)

<div class="callout"><strong>The parallel to junior developers is striking:</strong> the traditional <em>entry path</em> into a career is being flooded, while established professionals at the top are largely insulated.</div>

# Key Takeaways

## AI is not uniformly destroying jobs — it is *restructuring* them. Entry-level and routine work is most at risk. Judgment, care, and creativity are most resilient. The transition costs fall hardest on those just starting out.  This is not a future prospect.  It is happening now.
