## COURSE STRUCTURE

This course has been assembled from various sources. The Great Courses video series, "Understanding Artificial Intelligence: Of Minds and Machines" will be used, but not in its entirety. I have instead chosen to leverage other available video and text sources, to give a more in depth course. I have relied on Claude.AI to assist in the composition of the slides.

For each class session, the first hour will be presentation — videos and sometimes my lectures. We will try to save 30 minutes at the end of each session for discussion. I have also assembled some supporting material that may be useful to check out between class sessions.

We're going to begin with this video: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyyL0yDhr7I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyyL0yDhr7I). This explains some important concepts about large language models. What I think the video shows is that while LLMs have been trained on extensive syntactic information — how to assemble sentences that imitate related content on the Internet — it is not at all clear that LLMs have derived true semantic meaning. An LLM can use the words "tree" and "person" and "democracy" plausably, according to patterns that often convey useful information, but it does not have a world model. It does not really know what a tree or person or democracy is.

    <div class="card-meta">Daniel Kahneman  ·  2011</div>

    <div class="card-desc">Kahneman's landmark account of the two-system model of human thought: fast, intuitive, automatic responses (System 1) versus slow, deliberate, effortful reasoning (System 2).     <div class="card-meta">Antonio Damasio  ·  2025</div>
    <div class="card-rule"></div>
    <div class="card-desc">Damasio argues that consciousness is rooted in the body's representation of itself and its emotional states — not just abstract reasoning. A counterpoint to purely computational views of mind, and useful background for evaluating claims about AI sentience or feelings.</div>
