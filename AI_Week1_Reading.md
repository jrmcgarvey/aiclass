## Slide 5

SUGGESTED READING

The Coming Wave

Mustafa Suleyman  ·  2023

The book to read first. Suleyman --- an AI company co-founder --- argues that AI and synthetic biology constitute an unstoppable wave. Valuable and very dangerous, he writes. Describes specific regulatory steps society must take to avoid a dystopian outcome. Accessible and alarming.

START HERE

Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans

Melanie Mitchell  ·  2019

A clear, practitioner-written account of AI history, how it works, and where it was headed. Somewhat dated: Mitchell predicted fluent language generation and self-driving cars were probably decades away --- both arrived within a few years of publication. Still an excellent primer.

SUGGESTED READING

The Alignment Problem

Brian Christian  ·  2021

How do you make an AI actually do what you want --- not just the letter of your instructions, but their spirit? Examines the difficulty of specifying human values in a way machines can follow. We may remember Asimov\'s Three Laws of Robotics, but it turns out the problem is far harder.

## Slide 9

SUGGESTED READING

The Chaos Machine

Max Fisher  ·  2023

How recommendation algorithms --- designed to maximize user engagement --- end up amplifying outrage, disinformation, conspiracy beliefs, polarization, and real-world violence. A deeply reported account of how AI-driven feeds reshape societies.

SOCIAL MEDIA & HARM

AI Snake Oil

Narayanan & Kapoor  ·  2025

Argues that predictive AI has been wildly oversold. Covers facial recognition, recidivism scoring, hiring tools, and more --- areas where AI claims don\'t hold up to scrutiny. How hype leads to harmful deployments and what skeptical evaluation looks like.

## Slide 12

SUGGESTED READING

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman  ·  2011

Kahneman\'s landmark account of the two-system model of human thought: fast, intuitive, automatic responses (System 1) versus slow, deliberate, effortful reasoning (System 2). Fascinating to compare with how AI systems process information --- and where they differ from us.

HUMAN COGNITION

The Feeling of What Happens

Antonio Damasio  ·  2025

Damasio argues that consciousness is rooted in the body\'s representation of itself and its emotional states --- not just abstract reasoning. A counterpoint to purely computational views of mind, and useful background for evaluating claims about AI sentience or feelings.

## Slide 13

SUGGESTED READING

\"Minds, Brains, and Programs\"

John Searle  ·  1980 (paper)

Searle\'s famous thought experiment --- the Chinese Room --- argues that symbol manipulation alone cannot produce genuine understanding or consciousness. The instructor finds the argument \"mysterian\": it may show that syntax isn\'t sufficient for semantics, but doesn\'t tell us what consciousness actually is.

PHILOSOPHY OF MIND

The Conscious Mind

David Chalmers  ·  1996

Chalmers coins \"the hard problem of consciousness\": why there is something it is like to be a mind, not just information processing. The instructor hasn\'t read this one personally, and views Chalmers as another mysterian --- pointing at the mystery without resolving it.

## Slide 16

SUGGESTED READING

\"A Neural Probabilistic Language Model\"

Yoshua Bengio et al.  ·  2003 (paper)

One of the foundational papers establishing that neural networks could model language by learning word embeddings --- representing words as vectors in a continuous space. Dense and mathematical, but historically important. The instructor acknowledges not fully understanding it.

SEMINAL RESEARCH

\"Attention is All You Need\"

Vaswani et al.  ·  2017 (paper)

The paper that introduced the Transformer architecture --- the foundation of every large language model in use today, from GPT to Claude to Gemini. Highly technical, but worth knowing it exists and what it claims. The instructor notes: \'I can\'t really understand it, but it is very important.\'
