AI neural networks learn using back propagation and gradient descent.  This video explains that the brain learns differently, with a theory of how it works -- but it's pretty technical.

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UccvsYEp9yc>
This is an excellent introduction to neural networks and AI by Nobel prize winner Geoffrey Hinton.  The explanation is non-technical, and gives the history.  However, I (and others) strongly disagree with several of the claims.  First, Hinton claims that LLMs have real understanding.  Second, he claims that LLMs learn basically as humans do.  He speaks contemptuously of Noam Chomsky, who has argued that elements of syntax are hardwired the brain -- but while most brain scientists have differences with Chomsky, they do agree that human language capabilities are indeed hardwired, which is why kids are much more efficient at learning language than LLMs are.

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mkw0cPyKq6c>
This is a video from a group that has built a chatbot to convince people that some conspiracy theories are bogus.  They measure the extent to which they are able to affect people's beliefs, shich is significant but far from complete.  Now, is this good news, or bad news?  Mostly bad, I think.  Chatbots will be widely deployed to influence people, in many cases by bad actors.

Hinton, followed by LeCun and another guy, debating whether LLMs really understand.  Hinton says yes, LeCun says no.  LeCun says LLMs don't have a "world model", just language, and so they don't understand.  He says AIs have to be developed that observe with affect.  Maybe so, but adding the affect, that is, intent, to an AI is dangerous in my view.

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V8lhUqKqzUc>
Two very positive achievements by AI: AlphaFold, which figured out how amino acid sequences are folded into a protein, and AlphaGenome, work in progress to discover how non-coding human DNA affects health and growth.  Non-coding DNA used to be called junk DNA, because it doesn't build proteins, but it turns out to be very important in how genes are expressed.  The algorithms used for AlphaGo, the program that beat the world champion Go player, were repurposed for these projects.

Elon Musk chose to put no protection into Grok for this kind of abuse.  Maybe he thought it would build the popularity of his AI, and it has done that.
